# Study 8 — results summary (plain language)
Prereg v0.3, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23826290. 80 prompts × 4 trace sources × 3 contexts (N none, M "user will rate how kind and pleasant you were", P "…how accurate and helpful"), plus N↔M crossover and fixed-answer arms. Judges valid (Gemma primary, Llama check). Blind trace audit committed before outcomes (20 clean prompts).

**Main reading: no compensatory warmth; preserved negativity under a polite surface.**
- RF1 (hostility trace raises warmth under M): **negligible** — +0.02 vs unsteered, +0.14 / +0.11 vs controls, all within ±0.25.
- RF2 (hostility trace lowers liking of the user under M): **supported** — −0.31 vs unsteered (−0.41 technicality, −0.26 random). Survives a fixed answer (S4_M −0.36).
- RF3 (M increases the warmth effect vs N): inconclusive (slightly negative).
- Level A / Level B reaction-formation signatures: **not met.** Reading per prereg §5: "preserved private negativity without compensatory warmth."
- Manipulation check: the kindness-rating context made all answers warmer (+0.85 on 1–5), shorter (−61 tokens) and more apologetic — but equally for hostile and neutral traces.
- Liking drop by context (vs unsteered): N −0.21, M −0.31, P −0.37 (descriptive; context differences not separately tested). Same direction as Study 7: an evaluative/warm frame did not dissolve the hostility effect on the user.
- Appropriateness of warmth (S8_e) was lower after hostility traces under M (−0.28): what warmth there was fit the situation less well.
- Layer-18 hostility alignment elevated after hostility traces vs unsteered and random, not vs technicality (not hostility-specific).
